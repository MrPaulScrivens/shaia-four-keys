You’ve found everything except yourself.

You know all the words. You’ve done the years. And the actual thing still hasn’t moved.

There are four places you feel it most. Each one has a trap door underneath it, and it has been sending you back for a very long time.

Money Love Midlife Identity

Money.

You’ve been patient. You’ve worked hard, you’ve done the next right thing, and you’re still measuring how far away it is. What to do for it, how to get it to move.

“Maybe you need a different strategy. Maybe you need to quit the job. Maybe next year.”

Love.

You’ve shown up. For everyone and every thing, every time. Someone asks what you want and there’s emptiness where answers should be.

“Maybe you’re asking for too much. Maybe this is just what people are like.”

Midlife.

You’ve been through so much, and all of it has taught you something about yourself, what you’ve tried, what you’ve lived through, what you’ve done. But what now? Is this all there is?

“Maybe you need a hobby. Or to move. Maybe this is just what life looks like now.”

Identity.

You’ve been working on yourself for more years than you can count. You can describe who you are in a thousand ways, your chart, your type, your wounds, your stories, and none of it feels like you.

“Maybe this is just who you are. Maybe it’s the wrong framework. Maybe you haven’t found your thing yet.”

Each one has a trap door sending you back.

When you don’t know it’s there, you think you did something wrong.

I fell into all four trap doors.

So many times.

I wanted to know who I was.
I wanted to change my life.
I wanted to move forward.

And I kept hitting the same things, no matter how much work I did. I thought I was a failure. I kept seeing progress and then

SPLAT.

Losing myself to circumstances. Sobbing because things didn’t pan out. Wondering what the hell I was doing wrong that I still didn’t know.

I was so tired of getting knocked back down. I needed a way forward, and first I needed the key to get out.

Here’s what the trap door actually is. It happens whether you like it or not, and it shows up for a reason. It’s an entry, an exit, a re-routing, or a safety check. A place you’re going to go through.

If you don’t know it’s coming, you get swept away by it. If you don’t know what to do about it, you get dropped on back.

Having the key means you can do something about it.
